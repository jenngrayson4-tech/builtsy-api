What to Expect

What your first few sessions look like

There's often a lot of uncertainty about starting therapy — what to say, how it works, whether it's "supposed to" feel a certain way. Here's the honest version.

Session one is mostly a conversation. I'll ask about what's been going on, your background, and what you're hoping to get out of our work together. You don't need to prepare anything or "perform" being in distress.

Sessions two and three tend to feel a bit less structured — we're still getting to know each other, noticing what themes keep showing up, beginning to build the framework of our work. By session four or five, most clients report feeling a meaningful shift.

This is a relationship. It builds over time. The more honest you're able to be, the more useful it becomes.

Session 1–2

Getting to Know Each Other

Open conversation, history, goals. Low pressure, no agenda. Just showing up and beginning.

Session 3–6

Patterns Emerge, Work Begins

We start to identify the loops, the patterns, the places where real change is possible. This is where the work gets interesting.

Session 7+

Depth, Momentum, Change

Real shifts begin to consolidate. You start bringing new tools into your life. The work becomes something you carry with you.

A Closer Look

Deep Expertise, Personalized Care

Every specialty I offer is grounded in evidence-based practice and shaped by years of clinical experience. Here's a closer look at what each area of care involves.

Individual Therapy

For anyone ready to do the work

Individual therapy is a private, confidential space for you to explore what's going on beneath the surface. Whether you're navigating a specific challenge or simply sensing that something needs to shift, our work together will be tailored entirely to you.

Sessions are 50 minutes and held weekly or bi-weekly via telehealth. We'll build a treatment plan together in our first few sessions — flexible enough to evolve as you do.

CBT ACT Somatic Telehealth Weekly or Bi-Weekly

Anxiety & Burnout

For the chronically exhausted

Anxiety and burnout are among the most common — and most misunderstood — presentations I see. Many clients have been "managing" for years before seeking support, often not recognizing how much the chronic stress has been costing them.

We'll work to understand the root causes of your anxiety or burnout, reduce symptoms with evidence-based tools, and build a sustainable foundation for long-term wellbeing — not just crisis management.

Nervous System Regulation Mindfulness CBT Boundary Work

Life Transitions

For the in-between spaces

Career changes, relationship endings, moving, loss, becoming a parent, leaving a parent — life transitions don't always have tidy names, but they all share a common thread: the disorientation of not knowing yet who you'll be on the other side.

Therapy during transitions offers a grounding space to process grief, clarify values, and make meaning out of change — so you can move through it with intention, not just survive it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you take insurance?

I am currently out-of-network with all insurance plans, but I provide superbills after each session so you can seek reimbursement from your insurer directly. Many PPO plans reimburse a significant portion of out-of-network mental health costs. I'm happy to help you understand how to submit a claim.

How long does therapy take?

There's no one-size-fits-all answer — it depends on what you're working through and how you're feeling over time. Some clients work with me for a focused 8–12 weeks on a specific issue. Others choose to work together for a year or more on deeper patterns. We check in regularly about how the work is going and adjust accordingly.

Are sessions fully confidential?

Yes. Everything you share in therapy is protected by law and professional ethics. There are a small number of specific legal exceptions (such as imminent risk of harm) which I explain fully in our first session — but in practice, what you share stays between us.

What if I've never been to therapy before?

You're in good company — many of my clients come in having never done this before. First-time therapy can feel vulnerable and uncertain, and that's completely normal. Our first session is designed to be easy, conversational, and low-pressure. You don't need to know what to say or how therapy "works." That's my job.

What if I want to stop therapy?

You can stop therapy at any time — there's no obligation or penalty. In fact, it's considered healthy and part of the process to discuss this openly together. We can plan a thoughtful ending, or simply pause if you need a break. The door is always open to return.
