The origin story

This started as a mom trying to teach her kid
something real.

Builtsy didn't begin as a product idea. It began as a problem I couldn't solve any other way — how do you hand a teenager the tools to build something genuinely good, when even AI doesn't know the difference between a site that looks finished and one that actually works?

Where it started

AI is new. Really new.

My son Grayson wanted to learn how to build websites. He's sharp, motivated, and genuinely curious about technology. So I sat down to show him how to use AI to build something real — and I immediately hit a wall I didn't expect.

The gap between what AI can do and what a fifteen-year-old can ask for is enormous. Not because he wasn't capable — because the language of prompting is its own skill, and nobody's taught it yet. You can't just say "build me a website" and get something that works. You have to know what to ask for. And that knowledge doesn't exist anywhere in a form a teenager — or most adults — can actually use.

So I started building that form myself.

"AI-built sites were coming out without the foundation that makes them actually work. No schema. No robots.txt. No llms.txt. Beautiful on the surface — invisible everywhere it counts."

The hidden problem nobody was talking about.

The more I dug in, the more I found a second problem underneath the first. Even when Grayson got Claude to build something that looked great, the sites were missing the technical foundation that makes a website discoverable — by Google, by AI search engines, by anyone who matters.

No JSON-LD schema. No robots.txt that invited AI crawlers in. No llms.txt. No sitemap. The sites looked done but weren't done — and there was no standard, no checklist, no system that taught you what "actually done" meant.

I wanted Grayson learning to build things the right way from day one. Not shortcuts. Not "good enough." Real, professional, technically sound websites — because if he was going to charge clients real money, he needed to deliver real quality.

What we built

A system, not a shortcut.

What became Builtsy started as a prompt. A very long, very specific prompt built over months — tested, broken, fixed, tested again. A prompt that encoded everything I knew about what makes a website technically excellent, and translated it into something Claude could execute reliably every single time.

Then I turned it into Blueprints — structured forms that generate the prompt for you, so you don't have to know the language of prompting to get a professional result. You fill in what you know, and the system handles the rest.

Grayson used it. It worked. His first client site passed every audit on the first try. And it became something bigger than I planned.
